# Chapters 4 and 5: Frame for Approval

**Status:** approved by the author, 2026-10-02. All six decisions in section 4 were accepted as recommended.

**Method:** Ch5 is framed first, from its endings. Ch4 is then framed as the chapter that produces what those endings read. Nothing here is built until it is approved.

**Fixed by the author:**
- The crown at stake is a new one, independent of both Adalia and Valdrenne. It is made out of the corpse of an invaded kingdom, or out of a civil war or rebellion.
- The Crowned ending comes in 5-7% of runs.
- Ch4 has 3-4 acts with time skips.
- Heirs are mixed into the main story: mentorship, management, drama and pride.

---

## 1. The premise: the Western Crown

By the end of Ch3 three things are true:
- Valdrenne has a fourteen-year-old king, with the Constable (Gaucelin de Brésy) ruling in his name.
- Adalia has an ageing King, an impatient Prince, and an Earl of Carrow who sold the last war.
- The player holds land in the strip between them: Ormel or Marsalin on the Sauvemer march, or Kerval in the Armance.

**Recommendation.** The new crown is the **West**: the Armance peninsula and the Salt Coast (Sauvemer and its march), possibly with Vervais.

**Why the West:**
- It belongs fully to neither kingdom.
- It has its own ducal line: Jehanne of Armance and her heirs.
- Its money is salt, which both crowns tax and the south of Valdrenne already resents (the salt tithe).
- All three of the player's possible manors are inside it, so every run starts Ch5 standing on the board.

**How it comes apart.** Both crowns break at once, and nobody holds the middle:
- **Adalia: a succession war.** King Aldred dies around year 40. Edwin succeeds. Carrow, whose secret the player may hold, rises with the Old Baronage.
- **Valdrenne: a regency war.** The boy-king comes of age and tries to break the Constable. The Constable holds the west with the Brésy affinity.

This uses both of the author's routes: the corpse of an invaded kingdom (Valdrenne's west) and a civil war (Adalia's).

**Recommended name for the realm:** "the West", or "the Crown of Armance and the Salt". Its people call it *le Couchant*.

## 2. Chapter 5: the Crown (ages 42+, years 42-46)

**Shape:** four acts and a computed epilogue.

1. **The Estates of the West.** With both crowns at war, the lords, towns and salt-guilds of the West meet at Lannec to decide whom they obey.
   - The player sits in that assembly as one of its great lords.
   - Candidates for the crown: Jehanne's heir (the dynastic claim), an Adalian governor's candidate, a Brésy candidate, and possibly the player.
   - It is decided by votes bought, owed and won. Every faction standing and debt from Ch1-4 matters here.
2. **The War of the West.** Both crowns come for the West. There is one great campaign, built in the Ch2 style: back and forth, hard-won, with risk that pays off at a cost.
   - The player commands, does not just fight.
   - His heirs ride with him or hold his lands.
3. **Recognition.** A crown no one recognises is a rebellion. Recognition can come from:
   - the Church (the Bishop of Saint-Lys);
   - the Sarenzan banks, the Lanzi and Varesco houses;
   - one of the two kings, bought with a marriage, a province or a betrayal.
4. **The Reckoning of a Life.** Who holds what when the war ends. Then the epilogue, assembled from state: spouse, heirs, retinue, manors, key NPCs, the West.

### Endings matrix

| Ending | Hard gates | Main state read |
|---|---|---|
| **Crowned** (5-7%) | At least two of: (a) a claim, (b) the Estates' acclamation, (c) the war won with his host decisive. Also: (d) recognition from at least one outside power; (e) great-lord station; (f) alive with a living heir or a fertile marriage. | Claim (Armance blood by marriage or remarriage), standing with the Estates, war counters, recognition flags |
| **Kingmaker** | Decisive in the Estates or the war, then crowns someone else: Jehanne's heir, Edwin, the Valdrennish boy-king, or a Brésy | The same levers as Crowned, plus the choice to give it away |
| **Founder of a House** | Great lord; at least two holdings kept; a living adult heir; on the winning side, or neutral and intact | Holdings, heirs, side |
| **Diminished Lord** | Survives on the losing side, or neutral and squeezed; keeps one holding | Side, standing with the winners |
| **Exile** | Losing side, with no terms; alive | Where he has friends: Sarenza (Lanzi or Fiammetta), Hroswald (Rotbart), Caldmoor (Coll), Adalia or Valdrenne |
| **Ruin** | Attainted and landless, but alive; or broken by debt (Carrow's paper, the Lanzi) | Debts, enemies, Carrow |

Other routes stay open: a living wife is never a dead end. The Estates and conquest route is Crowned without a claim, and it needs recognition.

## 3. Chapter 4: the Great Lord (ages 32-42, years 33-42)

Its job is to produce the inputs Ch5 reads:
- station (great lord);
- holdings;
- a claim, or not;
- the party he stands with in each crown;
- standing in the West;
- adult or near-adult heirs with their own natures;
- the debts and secrets he holds over others, and the ones others hold over him.

### Acts
1. **The Second War** (years 33-35).
   - The great council at Wendmere opens the chapter, in his chosen party.
   - The truce ends. Adalia goes back into Valdrenne against the regency.
   - He commands a company of 60-200 men with named lieutenants: Davy, Tallis, Cobb, Rooke.
   - The Vervais secret is live: Carrow's treachery can surface on the field. Carrow holds back at a decisive moment unless the King or the player has pinned him.
   - One great battle, plus a siege or a raid.
   - **Outcome:** banneret status, and a second grant in the West.
2. **The Lord of Many Places** (years 36-38).
   - Several holdings: the first manor, plus his wife's land or his home place, plus the new grant.
   - Seasonal decisions across holdings, run through stewards he picks and can lose.
   - **Heirs' first growth period.** The eldest is 5-8. Fostering choice: page in a great house, kept at home, or the Church. The child's temperament shows. One small scene each for pride, worry and drama.
   - **Court politics.** King against Prince against Carrow. Plots and favours. The Vervais secret is spent or kept.
   - **A third child** is possible here, keeping the cap at three.
3. **The Fracture** (years 39-41).
   - King Aldred dies. Edwin is crowned, and Carrow rises.
   - In Valdrenne, the boy-king turns on the Constable.
   - The West starts acting for itself: salt-guilds withhold taxes, and Jehanne's line stirs.
   - **Heirs' second growth period.** The eldest is 10-13: squire or betrothal. The first real quarrel between father and child.
   - **Death of a parent figure:** his old master, his mother, or Pryce.
4. **The Summons to Lannec** (year 42).
   - The Estates of the West are called.
   - He arrives as what Ch4 made him.
   - Ch4 ends; Ch5 opens in that hall.

### Size and scope guard
- **Ch4:** about 55-65 scenes and about 80k words. A single run sees about 35.
- **Ch5:** about 40-50 scenes plus the epilogue builder, about 60k words.
- **The rule we agreed:** any Ch4 thread that no ending reads is cut. Every act above feeds a column of the endings matrix.

## 4. Decisions needed from the author
1. **The crown.** Do you approve the West (Armance and the Salt Coast) as the new realm, coming apart under both the Adalian succession war and the Valdrennish regency war? The alternative is a northern crown out of an Adalian rebellion with Caldmoor, which fits the player's lands badly.
2. **Aldred dies around year 40.** Is Edwin the next king, with Carrow rising against him?
3. **Candidate #12.** Is she Jehanne's granddaughter, the Armance heiress, as the marriage claim?
4. **Heir risk in Ch4.** Can an heir die in the Second War or the Fracture? Recommendation: yes, rarely, and only through choices the player made (sending a 13-year-old squire to war).
5. **The player's own death in Ch5.** If he dies, does the game continue as his heir for the epilogue choices? Recommendation: no. His death is an ending, and the heirs' fates are told in the epilogue.
6. **Vervais in the West.** Is it part of the new crown? Recommendation: no. Keep it as a third party that sells to whoever wins, which fits Lothaire.
