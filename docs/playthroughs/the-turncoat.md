# The Turncoat

Background: Household servant's son. Seed 3.

## The Destrier

*Spring, year 9 of King Aldred*

Ravell Hall, the stable yard, a raw spring morning.

You sleep in the kennels with the lymers, and you smell like them. Your mother dresses Lady Ravell. You eat in the kitchen with the scullions and know more about the family than the family thinks. You know, for one thing, who the heir's real father is. You heard it through a privy wall two years ago and have told no one.

Aymer Ravell is nine, a year older than you, and the lord's second son. He wants to sit on his father's destrier, Bayard, a stallion that has killed one groom. He wants you to saddle it. Master Ranulf is at the far end of the yard.

- Saddle Bayard for him and hold the head. *(Even)*
- Refuse him, and fetch Master Ranulf.
- Saddle him, and tell Aymer you will take the blame if you are caught.

**Chose:** Saddle him, and tell Aymer you will take the blame if you are caught.

You are caught. Master Ranulf beats you with a stirrup leather in front of the grooms. Aymer watches from the gate and says nothing. That night he brings you a honey cake from the high table and says he owes you. He means it, for now.

*Injury: Bruised ribs · Aymer Ravell's affection +2 · Aymer Ravell's respect +2 · Honor +1 · Bruised ribs has healed*

## The Wet Year

*Summer, year 11 of King Aldred*

You are 10.

It has rained since Whitsun. The barley lies flat in the fields and sprouts in the ear. Sheep cough in the folds. By Lammas, bread costs three times what it did, and the bread is half bean flour and grit.

At Ravell Hall the high table still has three courses, and the scraps from it go to the poor at the gate. Not all the scraps reach the gate. Nell from the laundry has a mother and five brothers in the village.

- Take a loaf from the high table scraps to Nell's mother before it reaches the gate.
- Set snares in Lord Ravell's chase after dark. *(Favorable)*
- Serve at the high table, where nobody is hungry, and keep your eyes down.
- Get into the tithe barn at night and fill a sack. *(Even)*

**Chose:** Set snares in Lord Ravell's chase after dark. (Favorable: success)

Two coneys the first week, a hare the second. You learn which paths the forester walks and when. Nobody at home asks where the meat comes from.

*Woodcraft +1*

## What a Boy Learns

*Summer, year 12 of King Aldred*

You are 11. The harvest came in at last, thin but enough.

A boy of your sort has perhaps four years before he is a man and the shape of his life is set. There is time to get good at one thing, if someone will teach it.

Father Benet, the chaplain, teaches Aymer his letters and lets the servants' boys sit at the back. Sir Bertram Oakes teaches the household's young men at the pell every morning.

Whatever you choose, something else goes untaught.

- Learn your letters, Latin and Adalian, properly.
- Learn to fight with sword and buckler.
- Learn how gentlefolk eat, speak and stand, by serving at their tables.
- Learn horses and hounds, riding and the chase.

**Chose:** Learn how gentlefolk eat, speak and stand, by serving at their tables.

You learn to carve a capon in the proper joints, pour without spilling, kneel on the correct knee, and never speak first. It feels like learning to act a part. You suspect that is what it is.

*Courtesy +2*

## Aymer Ravell

*Spring, year 14 of King Aldred*

You are 13.

Aymer Ravell is fourteen and has guests: two squires from Carrow's household. To impress them he has told you to fetch his falcon, and then told them you stole it, for the joke. They are laughing. One of them is not joking.

- Tell the Carrow squire to his face that you are no thief, and stand there. *(Even)*
- Find the falcon where Aymer really hid it, and let the Carrow boys watch you do it. *(Even)*
- Laugh along with Aymer, take the blame, and talk to him alone later. *(Even)*
- Do nothing. Let it pass and keep your head down.

**Chose:** Find the falcon where Aymer really hid it, and let the Carrow boys watch you do it. (Even: success)

The falcon is in Aymer's own chest. The Carrow boys find that funny, at Aymer's cost. Aymer never quite forgives you for being clever in front of guests.

*Aymer Ravell's affection −2 · Aymer Ravell's respect +1 · Intrigue +1*

## Fourteen

*Autumn, year 15 of King Aldred*

You are 14.

Fourteen is old enough to be bound apprentice, or put to the plough, or sent for a soldier. It is the age when a boy of your sort is either taken into somebody's service or left where he was born.

Lord Ravell's household is choosing its pages and grooms for the year. Guests are coming for the autumn hunting, Sir Ancel Brome among them.

- Go and see what can be made of it.

**Chose:** Go and see what can be made of it.

## The Autumn Hunting

*Autumn, year 15 of King Aldred*

The Ravell household fills for the autumn hunting: knights, their wives, their hounds, their quarrels. Sir Ancel Brome comes down from the March with a lame horse and a bad temper. Sir Hamon Darrell comes from Ashby with neither horse nor money enough.

This is the year your mother has said you must be placed above the stables or not at all. Lady Ravell is watching you more closely than she used to. You wonder sometimes whether she guesses what you know.

Aymer is to go as squire to Sir Hamon in the spring. He has said, twice, that he wants you with him.

- Show Sir Ancel what the Ravell hounds and hawks can do in your hands. *(Favorable)*
- Ask Aymer to ask his father for you, as his fellow squire. *(Favorable)*
- Go with the hunt on the boar day, on foot, with a spear. *(Risky)*
- Go to Lady Ravell alone, and tell her what you heard through the privy wall. *(Even)*
- Keep your head down and hope to be noticed.

**Chose:** Go to Lady Ravell alone, and tell her what you heard through the privy wall. (Even: success)

She hears you out without moving. Then she asks what you want. You tell her. A week later Sir Ancel Brome is asked to take a boy from the household to the March, a long way from the hall. Lady Ravell never looks at you again if she can help it. Your mother does not know why.

*Station: Retainer · Lady Eleanor Ravell's affection −6 · Lady Eleanor Ravell's respect +2 · Ruthlessness +2*

## Leaving

*Spring, year 16 of King Aldred*

You are 15, and you leave in the morning.

You go as a knight's boy, which is not yet a squire and is a long way from a knight. You will carry, clean, fetch, serve, and be hit.

Your mother straightens your collar and tells you not to shame her. She means: do not shame her in front of the lady. Nell is hanging sheets in the drying-yard and has stopped hanging them.

- Go to Nell and tell her you will come back.
- Promise your mother you will never speak of the secret again, to anyone.
- Leave before dawn, before anyone can make you promise anything.

**Chose:** Leave before dawn, before anyone can make you promise anything.

The road is empty and the frost is hard. By the time the village wakes you are two miles gone. Nobody will say you broke a promise. Nobody will say you made one, either.

## Ravell Hall

*Spring, year 16 of King Aldred*

You are 15.

Ravell Hall is a stone keep with a timber hall built against it, a moat that stinks in summer, and more people than your whole village: knights, squires, grooms, cooks, laundresses, clerks, priests, dogs. Lord Thurstan Ravell holds it from the King, and holds four knights' fees under him. Sir Hamon Darrell and Sir Ancel Brome are two of them.

Sir Ancel meets you in the yard without getting down from his horse. "You're late," he says. You are not late.

You will sleep in the squires' loft over the great chamber with four others. The eldest, Giles Marrick, looks at your boots, then your hands, then your face, and decides something.

- Kneel to your master in the yard, properly, as if you had been doing it all your life. *(Even)*
- Go straight to the stables and make yourself useful before anyone tells you to.
- Find someone in the hall who might tell you how things are done here.

**Chose:** Find someone in the hall who might tell you how things are done here.

Most people are too busy. One is not. A young woman in a good grey gown, carrying a basket of thread for the lady, stops and tells you where to wash, where to eat, which steward to avoid, and that Lady Ravell can hear through walls. Her name is Isabel Sayer. She waits on Lady Ravell, and she is, she says, as poor as you are, only better dressed.

## The High Table

*Summer, year 16 of King Aldred*

Midsummer feast. The Earl of Carrow's captain, Sir Walter Pryce, is at the high table with Lord Ravell, and your master is to serve the lord's own dish. That means you are. A swan, carved in its proper joints, laid on trenchers in the proper order, with the proper words. Giles Marrick has done it before. He has offered to help you, which means he will not.

- Serve slowly and correctly, every joint in its place. *(Even)*
- Serve it fast and plain, the way a hungry man wants to be served. *(Even)*
- Watch the others closely and do exactly what the best of them does. *(Favorable)*

**Chose:** Watch the others closely and do exactly what the best of them does. (Favorable: success)

You copy Aymer Ravell, not Giles, because Aymer was taught by his mother's carver and Giles was taught by Giles. It works.

*Courtesy +1 · Station: Squire*

## Giles Marrick

*Autumn, year 16 of King Aldred*

At Michaelmas your master named you his squire before the household. It means you are fed at the second table, carry his harness, sleep across his door when he travels, and are beaten by him rather than by grooms.

Giles Marrick is seventeen, the son of a Carrow knight, and he has decided you are a churl in a borrowed coat. He calls you the household servant's son in front of the other squires. He has put your master's best harness in the moat and told the marshal you did it.

The other squires watch to see what you do.

- Challenge him at the pell, in front of Sir Bertram Oakes. *(Risky)*
- Arrange for Giles to be found where he should not be. *(Favorable)*
- Win over the other squires, one at a time, until Giles is the one who stands alone. *(Risky)*
- ~~Stand the whole loft a cask of Gascon wine on Saint Michael's night.~~ *(Requires Coin 5s)*
- Take it. Do your duties better than he does his, and let that be your answer.

**Chose:** Arrange for Giles to be found where he should not be. (Favorable: success)

Giles is found by Lady Ravell's steward in the buttery with a jug of the lord's Gascon wine and no memory of how he got there. He is sent home to his father for a season. Nobody can prove anything. Giles does not need proof.

*Giles Marrick's affection −5 · Ruthlessness +1 · Intrigue +1*

## Aymer

*Autumn, year 16 of King Aldred*

> Consequence of: Find the falcon where Aymer really hid it, and let the Carrow boys watch you do it.

Aymer Ravell is a squire now, two years ahead of you in everything that counts here. He has not forgotten the falcon. In front of the household he is careful. Alone, he is not.

- Make it right with him, once and for all. *(Even)*
- Make it clear, in front of others, who stands higher now. *(Even)*
- Let it be known what Aymer owes, and to whom.
- Leave it. You have work to do.

**Chose:** Make it right with him, once and for all. (Even: failure)

He hears you out and laughs. Some things do not get made right.

*Aymer Ravell's affection −1*

## The Cunning Woman

*Autumn, year 16 of King Aldred*

Old Mother Rook lives in the last house in Ravell village, past the pound. She sells charms against fever, love-philtres, and lead flasks of water from Saint Ebba's well that turn blades, she says. The chaplain calls her a fraud. The chaplain's own groom buys from her.

- Buy a flask of Saint Ebba's water. Fourpence.
- Let her read your hand.
- Leave her to her charms.

**Chose:** Buy a flask of Saint Ebba's water. Fourpence.

It is cold in your hand and heavier than water should be. You carry it on a cord round your neck after that. You never quite decide whether you believe in it. You never take it off.

*Coin −4d · Gained: Lead ampulla · Gained: Carries a saint's token*

## Winter at the Pell

*Winter, year 16 of King Aldred*

Snow on the moat, ice on the trough, and the hall full of smoke from Martinmas to Candlemas.

Winter is when a household learns. Sir Bertram Oakes takes the young men to the pell every morning before Mass, whatever the weather. Master Ranulf breaks the new horses in the covered ride. Father Benet keeps the lord's accounts and letters in a cold room over the chapel, and will let a boy sit in if he holds his tongue. Lady Ravell keeps her own hall, where young men learn to be tolerable company or are not invited back.

You have the winter. You cannot be everywhere.

- Sir Bertram's pell, every morning, until your arms stop shaking.
- The covered ride with Master Ranulf and the young horses.
- Father Benet's cold room, and the lord's accounts.
- Lady Ravell's hall, where the young men learn to be company.

**Chose:** The covered ride with Master Ranulf and the young horses.

You are thrown nine times before Candlemas. By Lady Day you can sit a horse that does not want you, and turn it with your knees while your hands do something else.

*Riding +2 · Master Ranulf's affection +1*

## Sir Ancel's Cattle

*Summer, year 17 of King Aldred*

Brome Tower is three days north of Ravell, on the March, where the King's peace is a rumour. In June forty of Sir Ancel's cattle are driven off in the night, and his herdsman is found in the burn with his head stove in.

Everyone knows who did it. Sir Godric Thorne holds the next manor down the valley and has been at law with Sir Ancel for eleven years over a mill. Sir Ancel says he will ride to Thorne's mill tonight and burn it with Thorne's miller inside.

Lord Ravell has written that there will be no private war on the March this year, by the King's order. Sir Ancel has used the letter to light the fire.

- Ride out at first light and follow the cattle yourself. *(Favorable)*
- ~~Offer to write out Sir Ancel's plaint to Lord Ravell's court instead.~~ *(Risky, Requires: can read)*
- Arm yourself and ride with him to Thorne's mill. *(Even)*
- Hold the horses and keep your own counsel.

**Chose:** Hold the horses and keep your own counsel.

Sir Ancel rides without you and comes back at dawn smelling of smoke, with nothing to say. The mill is gone. So is any hope of settling the thing by law.

## The Herb Garden

*Summer, year 17 of King Aldred*

Isabel Sayer is in Lady Ravell's herb garden cutting lavender, alone, which she is not supposed to be. She tells you that Lady Ravell is at prayers and will be for an hour, and that you should not be here, and makes room for you on the bench.

Her father, she says, is a knight with a hundred acres and seven daughters. She is the fourth. He is looking for husbands for all of them, and is not fussy.

- Sit and talk, and keep your hands to yourself.
- Kiss her. *(Favorable)*
- Tell her you should not be here, and go.

**Chose:** Sit and talk, and keep your hands to yourself.

You talk about her father, and yours, and money, and the impossibility of everything. It is the most honest hour you have had at Ravell. When the chapel bell goes she stands up and says, "Well," and goes, and you sit there a while longer.

*Isabel Sayer's affection +2*

## Twelfth Night

*Winter, year 17 of King Aldred*

You are 16.

Twelfth Night at Ravell Hall. The great hall is hung with green, there are minstrels from Wendham, and the Lord of Misrule is a fat cook in a paper crown. Everyone drinks too much. The rules are loosened, not lifted.

Lady Ravell's women dance the carol in a ring and choose partners from the young men. Isabel Sayer is among them. So is Lady Maud de Lisle, Lord Ravell's ward, fifteen, heiress of the barony of Lisle, who is watched by everyone and spoken to by almost no one. The Wendham burgesses have a table below the salt, with their wives and daughters. Master Osbert Fuller's daughter Cecily is the one doing her father's talking for him.

At the top table the knights are drinking a health to the King's claim on Valdrenne, whatever that is going to mean.

- Join the carol and dance with Isabel Sayer. *(Favorable)*
- Carry Lady Maud's cup when her own page is drunk, and listen to her. *(Even)*
- Go below the salt and sit with the Wendham burgesses. *(Even)*
- Drink with the knights at the top table, and keep up. *(Even)*
- Slip out to the chapel and write to Nell at home.

**Chose:** Carry Lady Maud's cup when her own page is drunk, and listen to her. (Even: success)

She has been waiting all evening for someone to talk to who is not afraid of her guardian. She talks quickly and low. Lord Ravell means to sell her marriage to the Earl of Carrow for his younger son. She has met the younger son. She would sooner take the veil. Then she remembers who you are and stops. "You will not repeat that," she says. It is an order and a question.

## The Leven Ford

*Summer, year 18 of King Aldred*

Dawn on the fourth day, in a cold mist off the river. Black Ewan's men are driving the cattle across the Leven ford, slowly, because cattle will not be hurried through water. Eighty men, perhaps more, half of them on the far bank already. They have not seen you.

Sir Ancel draws up the horse behind a fold of ground. Will Cobb has the archers strung and waiting in the alders. Sir Hamon sits the Ravell banner and looks old in the grey light. Beside him is Sir Hugh Wyck, a knight of Lord Ravell's from the next valley, who smiles at you as if this were a hunt.

Sir Ancel says the horse will go in when the archers have loosed twice. That is all the plan there is.

You have no armour. A ford fight is a lottery for a man without it.

- Take a spear and go into the ford with the horse. *(Even, MORTAL DANGER)*
- Take two of Cobb's men upstream to the second ford and get behind the herd. *(Favorable)*
- Get down into the alders with Cobb's archers and make them shoot faster. *(Risky)*
- Wade out and shout for a parley. The cattle, for their lives. *(Risky)*
- Stay with the horses, as you were told.

**Chose:** Take two of Cobb's men upstream to the second ford and get behind the herd. (Favorable: success)

The second ford is waist-deep and colder than anything you have known. You come out of the alders behind the drovers, shouting, and the cattle turn back into the river on top of Ewan's own men. In the confusion you find a boy holding the raiders' horses, and he gives himself up rather than be trampled. Sir Ancel says, after, that it was the cattle that won the fight, and that someone put them there.

*Renown +2 · Sir Ancel Brome's respect +3*

## Coll of Glenhallow

*Summer, year 18 of King Aldred*

The prisoner is sixteen, red-haired, and frightened, though he is trying not to be. His name is Coll. He is Black Ewan's sister's son. Ewan will pay for him, or will burn half the March to get him back, or both.

Sir Ancel's men want to hang him from the nearest tree, for the priest at the lychgate. Lord Ravell wants him ransomed. Sir Ancel says he is yours to decide, since you took him, and watches to see what you will do.

- Hold him for ransom, properly, through Lord Ravell.
- Hand him over to your master, and let him take the credit and the money.
- Let the men hang him, for the priest at the lychgate.
- Cut his bonds in the night and let him go. *(Favorable)*

**Chose:** Cut his bonds in the night and let him go. (Favorable: success)

He goes over the wall without a word and stops at the top to look back at you. In the morning nobody can say how the knots came loose. Sir Ancel looks at you a long time and decides not to ask.

*Honor +1 · Caldmoor standing +3 · Coll's affection +5 · Lord Thurstan Ravell's respect +2*

## Home from the Leven

*Summer, year 18 of King Aldred*

Lord Ravell meets the column at the gate. He counts the cattle, then the men, then the dead. One knight, Sir Hugh Wyck. Two archers. A groom nobody can name.

He stops his horse beside you. "The ford," he says. "That was you." It is not a question. Afterwards, in the hall, he sends you a cup of his own wine from the high table, which every man in the hall sees.

- Go back to your duties.

**Chose:** Go back to your duties.

There is harness to mend and horses to doctor and a ford to stop dreaming about. You do all three, slowly.

## The King at Ravell

*Summer, year 19 of King Aldred*

You are 18.

King Aldred comes to Ravell on his summer progress with three hundred people and eats the house bare in four days. He is thirty-four, thin, quick, with his mother's dark Valdrennish eyes. He talks about Valdrenne at every meal. The crown there is his by right, he says, through his mother, and the peers of Valdrenne gave it to a cousin because they were afraid of an Adalian king. He does not say what he means to do about it. Everyone at the high table knows.

The Earl of Carrow rides in on the second day with Sir Walter Pryce and forty men. The Earl does not like the King's war; he likes it less that the King has not asked him to pay for it yet. You notice that Carrow's younger son is seated next to Lady Maud de Lisle. She looks at her plate the whole meal.

The household is in a fever. Squires are everywhere at once. For three days it is possible, just, for anyone to be seen by anyone.

- Get yourself near the King. Hold his stirrup, carry his cup, be there. *(Even)*
- Watch the King's household for whoever is watching it. *(Favorable)*
- Make yourself known to Sir Walter Pryce, who is hiring men for the war. *(Even)*
- Get Lady Maud's petition into the King's hands, past her guardian. *(Even)*
- Keep to your master's side and do your work well.

**Chose:** Get Lady Maud's petition into the King's hands, past her guardian. (Even: success)

The petition goes in among the King's letters by way of a clerk who owes Father Benet money. It asks the King to take her wardship into his own hand. On the last morning, at Mass, the King announces that he will do exactly that. Lord Ravell smiles and loses a thousand marks. He does not know how. Lady Maud does, and looks at you across the chapel once, for a long time.

*Crown standing +1*

## The Claim

*Winter, year 20 of King Aldred*

You are 19.

At Christmas the heralds ride through every shire town in Adalia with the King's letters. Aldred, by the grace of God King of Adalia, is by right of his mother the true King of Valdrenne, and will take what is his. The Moot has granted a tenth and a fifteenth. Writs of array go out for archers. The great lords are to bring their companies to Saltcombe by Saint George's Day.

Lord Ravell will take forty lances and two hundred archers. Every knight of his is going. Every squire is going. The Earl of Carrow's company will be twice that, and Sir Walter Pryce means to come home from it a banneret.

For a squire, there is one question now. Whether you cross the Narrow Sea with spurs on your heels, or without them.

- Make ready.

**Chose:** Make ready.

## Horse and Harness

*Winter, year 20 of King Aldred*

A knight must have a warhorse, a riding horse, a harness of plate, a squire of his own, and the means to keep all of them for a summer's campaign. A squire with none of those things can be knighted, in law. In practice, nobody will dub a man who cannot afford to be one. It shames the man who dubs him.

You have £4. You have a horse of your own.

- ~~Accept what your master offers: his spare harness and a horse from his string.~~ *(Requires: your master's affection)*
- Accept a fee from Lord Ravell, ten pounds a year for life, for your service.
- ~~Ride your own horse, in your own prize harness.~~ *(Requires: the prize harness and a horse of your own)*
- Buy a second-hand harness and a horse in Wendham. Four pounds.
- ~~Borrow twenty pounds from the Lanzi bank against what you will win in Valdrenne.~~ *(Requires: a way in at the Lanzi bank)*
- ~~Accept Dame Joan Wyck's offer of her late husband's harness and horse.~~ *(Requires: Dame Joan's regard)*
- Accept Sir Walter Pryce's offer of horse and harness from the Earl of Carrow, and wear the Earl's livery.
- You cannot. Go to the war as you are, and win it there.

**Chose:** Accept Sir Walter Pryce's offer of horse and harness from the Earl of Carrow, and wear the Earl's livery.

The Earl of Carrow equips forty young men this spring, and asks only that they wear his badge, the black boar, and remember who paid for their horses. Lord Ravell sees the boar on your coat in the yard and says nothing at all, which is how lords say a great deal.

*Lord Thurstan Ravell's affection −3 · Sir Walter Pryce's affection +1 · Great Nobles standing +1 · Horses +1*

## The Accolade

*Winter, year 20 of King Aldred*

You have the means. Now you need a knight willing to give you the accolade, and to stand for you afterwards, because a knight dubbed by nobody in particular is nobody in particular.

There are knights at Ravell who will not stand in the same chapel as you.

- ~~Ask your master to knight you.~~ *(Requires: your master's respect, and some renown)*
- ~~Accept Lord Ravell's offer to knight you himself, before the household.~~ *(Requires: Lord Ravell's favour, and renown)*
- ~~Go to Saltcombe, where the King will knight a batch of squires before the army sails.~~ *(Requires: the King's notice, and renown)*
- Kneel to Sir Walter Pryce, in the Earl of Carrow's colours.
- Not yet. Go to war as a man-at-arms under your master's banner, and win your spurs in the field.

**Chose:** Kneel to Sir Walter Pryce, in the Earl of Carrow's colours.

Sir Walter dubs you in the Earl's chapel at Carrow, with eleven others, all in the black boar. It is quick and businesslike. Afterwards he hands each of you an indenture for three years' service to the Earl. Everyone signs. That was always the price.

*Station: Knight · Sir Walter Pryce's respect +1 · Knights standing −1*

## The Vigil

*Winter, year 20 of King Aldred*

The night before, you keep vigil in the chapel in a white shirt, with your sword on the altar. You are supposed to pray until dawn and think about the duties of a knight: to defend the Church, the weak, the widow, and his lord.

In the morning you are bathed, dressed, armed, and struck once on the neck. It is harder than you expected. Then you are Sir Hal, and the world is exactly the same, except that it is not.

- Pray, as you are meant to, until dawn.
- Let your friends smuggle in a flask and keep you company.
- Sit with your sword and think about what you will do with it.

**Chose:** Sit with your sword and think about what you will do with it.

You count, as you always have. What a knight's fee is worth. What a ransom is worth. What a manor is worth. How many men your age will come back from Valdrenne richer than they went. By dawn you have a plan, of sorts. It has a great many dead men in it, and you are not one of them.

*Tactics +1 · Ruthlessness +1*

## Before the Ships

*Spring, year 21 of King Aldred*

You are 20. The army musters at Saltcombe in a week.

There is time for one thing before you go. Perhaps two, if you do not sleep.

Isabel Sayer is at Ravell with Lady Ravell's household. Lady Maud de Lisle is at Wendmere in the King's wardship, and has written to you twice.

- Find Isabel Sayer and give her your token.
- Ride to Wendmere and see Lady Maud, however briefly.
- Go home to Ravell's laundry yard one last time.
- Spend the night in the chapel. Nobody can say you went unshriven.
- Spend the last night in the alehouse with the men.

**Chose:** Ride to Wendmere and see Lady Maud, however briefly.

You are allowed a quarter-hour in a parlour with a nun in the corner. Maud gives you her glove under the nun's nose. She says the King has promised her marriage to nobody yet, and that she has not promised herself to anybody either. She looks at you when she says it.

*Gained: A keepsake*

## Saltcombe

You are 20.

Saltcombe harbour is so full of ships that you could walk across it on their decks. Horses are being slung aboard in canvas with their legs kicking. Archers are counting arrows. A Sarenzan galley sits at the mouth of the harbour like a crow on a fence, waiting to see who wins.

You go aboard as Sir Hal, with a horse below decks and a harness in a barrel, and no idea what any of it will be worth in a month.

There are men on this ship who know what you did to your old master. They will be beside you in the line.

End of Chapter 1. The war is next.
