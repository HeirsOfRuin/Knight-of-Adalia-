# Canon Sheet

The single reference for world, factions, geography and naming. Every name is provisional until Phase 3 sign-off. If content contradicts this file, this file wins and the content gets fixed. Items needing historical review are in `TODO.md`.

## The world in one paragraph
Two kingdoms share a cold, wet island. Adalia, in the south, is rich in wool and short of money. Caldmoor, in the highlands, is poor and fierce. Across the Narrow Sea lies Valdrenne, the largest and richest realm in the known world. Its last king of the old line died without a son. The peers of Valdrenne crowned his cousin. They passed over the dead king's sister's son: King Aldred of Adalia. Aldred has a claim and an empty treasury. Valdrenne has the men, the castles and the money. Hroswald, a patchwork of princes to the east, sells soldiers to anyone. Sarenza, a league of southern ports, lends money to everyone and is owed by most.

## Geography
- **The island** (Adalians just call it "the island"). Adalia covers the southern two-thirds, Caldmoor the highland north. **The March** is the disputed border country between them: tower houses, cattle raids, divided families.
- **The Narrow Sea** separates the island from Valdrenne. The crossing takes a day or two with a fair wind and can take weeks without one.
- **Adalia**
  - **Wendmere**: the capital, on a tidal river.
  - **Saltcombe**: chief port and embarkation point.
  - **Wendham**: a wool market town in the south midlands.
  - **Ashby**: a manor near Wendham, held by Sir Hamon Darrell.
  - **Ravell Hall**: seat of Lord Thurstan Ravell, about a day's ride from Wendham.
- **Valdrenne**
  - **Cordelle**: the capital, inland on a great river.
  - **The Armance**: a western peninsula, a duchy with its own disputed succession.
  - **Vervais**: a northeastern duchy of weaving towns. They live on Adalian wool.
  - **The Midi**: the rich south. Wine and salt.
- **Sauvemer**: a walled port on a spit between the sea and the salt marshes, two days up the coast from the Armance. Adalian from year 23. **Ormel** is a poor marsh manor behind it. **Marsalin** is the salt manor on the edge of Les Salines, with a stone house on the dyke and the battle's sluice-gates.
  - **Kerval**: a manor of orchards and good farmland in the Armance hills behind Lannec, held of the King with the Duchess's assent.
  - **The Lisonne** is the river between Amaury's lands and Vervais. **The Aube** crosses the road back west at **Grisolles**.
- **Hroswald**: forest, mountains and mining towns east of Valdrenne. The **Ostmark** is its eastern frontier.
- **Sarenza**: a league of ports on the southern sea. Sarenza is the chief city, with **Vellagio** and **Calvi Porto** beside it.

## Adalia
- **Government:** a feudal monarchy. The king rules with a Council. Taxes need the consent of **the Moot**, an assembly of lords, knights of the shire and burgesses. The Crown is poor. It depends on the wool custom and on Sarenzan loans.
- **Military**
  - Strengths: longbow archers drawn from the shire levies and indentured companies; ships; captains experienced from the March.
  - Weaknesses: few heavy horse, no money for long campaigns, poor siege train.
- **Economy:** raw wool, which goes to Vervais through the **staple** at Saltcombe. Tin, hides, salt fish.
- **Internal factions**
  - **The King's party:** household knights and royal officials. They want the war.
  - **The Old Baronage**, led by the Earl of Carrow. They want the Crown kept poor and the Moot strong. They will fight in the war if it pays.
  - **The Marcher lords**, who will not leave the border bare while Caldmoor sits behind it.
  - **The towns.** They pay and expect to be heard.
- **Attitudes**

| Toward | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Valdrenne | Claimant and rival |
| Caldmoor | Old enemy |
| Hroswald | Possible paid ally |
| Sarenza | Creditor; resented |

- **NPCs**
  - **Aldred II**, King, 24 at game start. Clever, short of money, proud of his mother's blood.
  - **Queen Ysolde**, Queen Mother. Valdrennish-born. She is the source of the claim and keeps a Valdrennish household.
  - **Edwin**, Prince of Adalia. The king's son, an infant at game start. He comes of age during Ch2-3.
  - **Osmund**, Earl of Carrow. Leads the Old Baronage. Shrewd and dangerous, not disloyal.
  - **Aylmer Stoke**, Bishop and Chancellor. Runs the money.
  - **Lord Thurstan Ravell.** A middling baron; master of the servant background's household.
  - **Sir Hamon Darrell**, knight of Ashby. Owes service to Lord Ravell.

## Valdrenne
- **Government:** a monarchy whose king is strong in theory and weak in practice. The great dukes hold their own courts, coin, and armies of vassals. The **Peers** (six dukes and six bishops) crowned Amaury and could, in principle, turn on him.
- **Military**
  - Strengths: a large heavy cavalry, more castles and walled towns than anywhere else, money to hire Sarenzan crossbowmen and Hroswald companies.
  - Weaknesses: slow levies, a chivalry that scorns foot soldiers, nobles who fight for glory and ransom rather than for orders.
- **Economy:** the richest land in the world. Grain, wine, and the **salt tithe**, a royal salt tax that the south resents.
- **Internal factions**
  - **The Sauvel loyalists**, the king's kin.
  - **The Armance succession quarrel.** Two claimants; one is backed by Cordelle, so Adalia may back the other.
  - **Vervais.** The weaving towns need Adalian wool more than they love their king.
  - **The southern lords**, who resent the salt tithe.
- **Attitudes**

| Toward | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Adalia | Upstart vassal |
| Caldmoor | Useful ally, bound by **the Old Bond** |
| Hroswald | Source of men |
| Sarenza | Lender and shipper |

- **NPCs**
  - **Amaury VI**, King, 40. Pious, careful, stubborn.
  - **Gaucelin de Brésy**, Constable of Valdrenne. A hard, competent soldier who despises archers.
  - **Duke Lothaire of Vervais.** Pragmatic. He will deal with whoever keeps his looms running.
  - **Duchess Jehanne of Armance.** Wife of the claimant Cordelle opposes. She fights on when her husband is taken.
  - **Bishop Évrard of Saint-Lys.** A peer of the realm, and the king's confessor.

## Caldmoor
- **Government:** a small, poor monarchy ruling through clan chiefs and thanes. The crown is often held by a child, with regents quarrelling.
- **Military**
  - Strengths: spearmen in close order (**the hedge**), hardy light horse for raiding, knowledge of hill country.
  - Weaknesses: no siege train, few archers, poor armour, cannot stay in the field long.
- **Economy:** cattle, hides, wool of poor grade, raiding.
- **Internal factions:** the regency council, the border clans who raid for themselves, and the northern Church.
- **Attitudes**

| Toward | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Adalia | Hereditary enemy |
| Valdrenne | Ally under the Old Bond; Valdrennish money pays for the raids |
| Others | Barely known |

- **NPCs**
  - **Dughal III**, King of Caldmoor, 12 at game start.
  - **Fearchar mac Lorn**, Thane of Ardmhor. Regent. Ambitious.
  - **Ewan Dubh of Glenhallow** ("Black Ewan"). A border raider who answers to no one.

## Hroswald
- **Government:** an elective crown. Seven **Electors**, some princes and some archbishops, choose the king. Princes, free cities and bishops rule their own lands.
- **Military:** a source of mercenary **free companies**. Heavy horse from the princes. Strong crossbows and handgonnes (crude, rare, unreliable).
- **Economy:** silver mines, river tolls, trading cities.
- **Internal factions:** the King-Elect's house, rival princes waiting for the next election, and the free cities.
- **Attitudes**

| Toward | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Valdrenne | Wary neighbour |
| Adalia | A paying customer, if it pays |
| Sarenza | Banker |

- **NPCs**
  - **Ottokar of Weltzen**, King-Elect. Old, and buying votes for his son.
  - **Hartmund**, Margrave of the Ostmark. A rival prince. Sells his support.
  - **Ulric Rotbart.** Captain of the Iron Company, a free company of about 800 men. Loyal to his contract and no further.

## Sarenza
- **Government:** a merchant republic league. The great families sit in **the Signory**, which elects a **Prior** for life. Each port governs itself and votes in the League.
- **Military:** war galleys, Sarenzan crossbowmen hired out by the company, and above all money: loans, ransoms, bribes.
- **Economy:** banking, shipping, spices and silk from the east, and brokering ransoms. Sarenza takes a fee for everything.
- **Internal factions:** the Varesco and Lanzi banking houses (rivals), the shipowners, and the guild of crossbowmen.
- **Attitudes:** backs whoever pays. Sarenza has lent to both Aldred and Amaury, and would be ruined if either defaulted.
- **NPCs**
  - **Ugolino Varesco**, Prior of the League.
  - **Matteo Lanzi**, head of the Lanzi bank. The burgess family's debt is owed to his house.
  - **Bertuccio Lanzi**, Matteo's nephew. Factor in Wendham.
  - **Nerio Spada**, galley captain and broker of crossbowmen.

## The Church
A single Church across all realms, under **the Pontiff**. For two generations the Pontiff has resided at **Saint-Lys** in Valdrenne, so Adalians suspect the Church leans Valdrennish. It is a cross-border institution, not a power. It is modelled through the Church reputation track and through clergy NPCs.
- **Archbishop Hugh Pelham of Wendmere.** Primate of Adalia, loyal to the Crown before the Pontiff.
- **Cardinal Orsino Brancale.** A Sarenzan by birth; papal legate, sent to broker peace.
- **Father Anselm.** Parish priest of Ashby. Taught the reeve's son his letters alongside his father.
- **Grey friars** preach in market squares, often against usury and taxes.

## Ravell country (Chapters 1 and earlier)
- **Ravell Hall.** Lord Thurstan Ravell's seat: a stone keep with a timber hall, a stinking moat, a tilt-yard, the chase, and Ravell village with its mill.
- **Lord Ravell** holds four knights' fees. Two of them belong to the protagonist's masters:
  - **Sir Hamon Darrell** of Ashby. Fifty-something, kind, careless with money, in debt to the Lanzi bank.
  - **Sir Ancel Brome** of Brome Tower on the March. About forty, hard, poor, at feud with Sir Godric Thorne.
- **The household**
  - Lady Eleanor Ravell. Keeps the household's secret: the heir's real father.
  - Aymer Ravell, the second son.
  - Lady Maud de Lisle, Lord Ravell's ward and heiress of Lisle.
  - Isabel Sayer, Lady Ravell's waiting-woman.
  - Sir Bertram Oakes, master-at-arms.
  - Master Ranulf, marshal.
  - Father Benet, chaplain and clerk.
  - Giles Marrick, a squire from a Carrow family.
  - Will Cobb, serjeant of Sir Ancel's archers.
- **Nearby**
  - **Wyck.** Dame Joan Wyck, widowed at the Leven ford, and her stepson Ralph.
  - **Thorne Hall.** Sir Godric Thorne.
  - **Ambry.** The abbey of Austin canons and the nuns, a day away.
- **Caldmoor border:** Black Ewan of Glenhallow and his nephew Coll. The **Leven** is the border river; its ford is the scene of the Ch1 raid.
- **The Earl of Carrow's company:** Sir Walter Pryce, captain. Badge: the black boar.
- **Carn Dubh.** A Caldmoor mill burned with people inside during the Marcher War. The archer background's father stood at its door.

## Low magic (rules for writers)
- **Alchemy:** practised by a few physicians and charlatans. Results are uncertain, expensive and sometimes poisonous.
- **Relics, pilgrim waters, charms:** common, cheap, and usually fake. A real one is indistinguishable from a fake one.
- **Curses and omens:** they work through belief and coincidence as far as anyone can tell. The text never confirms a supernatural cause.
- **Enchanted objects:** a handful in the whole world, each with a known price. Each is an item with a cost, never a general power.
- **In Chapter 1:** Mother Rook's flask of Saint Ebba's water gives +1 on lethal checks, labelled "Saint Ebba's water, or your faith in it." The game never says which.

**Titles**

- Caldmoor: King, Thane, chief.

## Chapter 3 events (years 26-32)
- **Year 26:** the Mottle. About a third of the people die.
- **Year 28, Lady Day:** the truce lapses into border raiding.
- **Year 31, Whitsun to Michaelmas:** the rising of the commons over the Ordinance.
  - At the Hythe Fields, across the river from Wendmere, the King grants charters.
  - Carrow's marshal cuts down the Coker captain while the King looks away.
  - The charters are revoked by Michaelmas, and the hangings run through August.
- **Year 32:** King Amaury of Valdrenne dies. His son is fourteen, and the Constable, Gaucelin de Brésy, rules in his name. The truce of Saint-Lys runs out the next Lady Day. The King calls a great council at Wendmere for Martinmas.
- **The Vervais betrayal (secret):** in year 21 the Earl of Carrow warned Duke Lothaire, by way of the Lanzi house, that the weaving towns would rise.

## Chapter 4 events (years 33-)
- **Year 32, Martinmas:** the great council at Wendmere votes money for a war in the West.
- **Year 33:** the truce lapses at Lady Day. The King lands at Lannec in June. Duchess Jehanne, 70, has an heir: her granddaughter Mahaut, 14, whose father died in Cordelle's prison. The army takes the Armance towns through the autumn, including Saint-Ferréol.
- **Year 34, May: Mortefontaine.** The Constable's men-at-arms attack the King's ridge on foot. The Earl of Carrow's battle stands still, or is made to move. The Constable's horse is broken in the dry stream. The outcome depends on the player's run, but the Constable always escapes to Cordelle, where he fights his own boy-king.
- **Year 35, Lent:** the King's grants. The player becomes a banneret.
