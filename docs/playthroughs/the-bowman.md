# The Bowman

Background: Veteran archer's son. Seed 5.

## The Butts

*Spring, year 9 of King Aldred*

Sunday after Mass, at the butts behind the church. Every man between sixteen and sixty is meant to shoot. Most of them drink.

Your father shoots alone at the far end, from one leg, because the other knee will not bear him. He still puts six in the clout from two hundred paces. Nobody watches him any more.

A man from his old company has walked over from the next parish. Will Cobb, with a grey beard and a new shirt. He and your father drink from the same jug and do not speak of the war, until Cobb says something about "the mill at Carn Dubh" and your father puts the jug down and tells you to go and fetch arrows.

- Ask your father what happened at Carn Dubh.
- Fetch the arrows, then shoot until your fingers bleed.
- Follow Will Cobb to the alehouse and listen to his stories.

**Chose:** Fetch the arrows, then shoot until your fingers bleed.

Your father's old bow is too heavy for you. You draw it anyway, a hand's breadth, then two. By dark the string has cut your fingers to the meat and your back is on fire. He binds your fingers with his own linen and says you will be crooked like him by twenty. He sounds pleased.

*Archery +1 · Hugh Fletcher's affection +2*

## The Wet Year

*Summer, year 11 of King Aldred*

You are 10.

It has rained since Whitsun. The barley lies flat in the fields and sprouts in the ear. Sheep cough in the folds. By Lammas, bread costs three times what it did, and the bread is half bean flour and grit.

Your father's pension has not been paid since Candlemas. There are four of you in the cottage and one sack of beans. Gib Shawe's uncle, the bailiff, eats meat every Sunday.

- Give half your beans to the widow two doors down, whose boys are worse off than you.
- Set snares in Lord Ravell's chase after dark. *(Even)*
- Hire yourself out to the carters on the Wendham road for a penny a day.
- Get into the tithe barn at night and fill a sack. *(Even)*

**Chose:** Give half your beans to the widow two doors down, whose boys are worse off than you.

You go hungry for it, and so does your family, and they let you know. The widow's boys follow you about after that, like hounds.

*Common Folk standing +2 · Piety +1 · Health −1 · Hugh Fletcher's respect +1*

## What a Boy Learns

*Summer, year 12 of King Aldred*

You are 11. The harvest came in at last, thin but enough.

A boy of your sort has perhaps four years before he is a man and the shape of his life is set. There is time to get good at one thing, if someone will teach it.

The priest teaches letters on feast days, if you can be spared from work, which you cannot. Will Cobb says he will teach you the sword and buckler if you carry his water.

Whatever you choose, something else goes untaught.

- Learn your letters, Latin and Adalian, properly.
- Learn to fight with sword and buckler.
- Learn how gentlefolk eat, speak and stand, by serving at their tables.
- Learn horses and hounds, riding and the chase.

**Chose:** Learn to fight with sword and buckler.

You learn to keep your feet under you and your eyes off the blade. You learn to take a blow on the buckler and not the hand. You learn that you are slower than you thought, and then a little faster.

*Arms +2 · Will Cobb's affection +2*

## Gib Shawe

*Spring, year 14 of King Aldred*

You are 13.

The bailiff is choosing one boy from the parish to be trained with the shire archers and paid for it. It is you or Gib Shawe, the bailiff's nephew. On Sunday the bailiff will watch you both shoot. Everyone knows how it will go.

- Shoot better than Gib. Nothing else. *(Favorable)*
- Find out what the bailiff owes, and to whom, before Sunday. *(Risky)*
- Go to Gib before Sunday and offer to split the work and the pay. *(Even)*
- Do nothing. Let it pass and keep your head down.

**Chose:** Shoot better than Gib. Nothing else. (Favorable: success)

You beat Gib by four arrows. The bailiff gives the place to Gib anyway. But the parish saw, and Will Cobb saw, and Gib knows what everyone saw.

*Gib Shawe's respect +2 · Gib Shawe's affection −3 · Common Folk standing +1*

## Fourteen

*Autumn, year 15 of King Aldred*

You are 14.

Fourteen is old enough to be bound apprentice, or put to the plough, or sent for a soldier. It is the age when a boy of your sort is either taken into somebody's service or left where he was born.

Sir Ancel Brome is holding a muster at Wendham for Lord Ravell's levy. Your father has put on his good shirt.

- Go and see what can be made of it.

**Chose:** Go and see what can be made of it.

## The Muster at Wendham

The muster is on the common outside Wendham. Three hundred men in a field, most of them with bows they have not drawn since last year. Clerks with rolls. A fat priest blessing everyone. Sir Ancel Brome sits his horse at the end of the line: a lean man of forty with a Marcher face, burned dark, missing half an ear.

Gib Shawe is there with the bailiff, and does not look at you.

Your father is leaning on his stick by the clerks' table. He has been watching Sir Ancel all morning. They have not spoken yet.

- Get into the shooting, and shoot where Sir Ancel can see. *(Favorable)*
- Let your father go to Sir Ancel and remind him of Harrow Ford. *(Risky)*
- Find the Ravell huntsmen at the edge of the field and offer yourself as a lymerer for the autumn hunting. *(Even)*
- Put your mark on the levy roll with the others and take what comes.

**Chose:** Put your mark on the levy roll with the others and take what comes.

The clerk writes your name for you. You are a shire archer now, for what that pays.

## The Lower Road

No knight has taken you. That is the end of one road.

It is not the end of service. A great household needs grooms and kitchen boys and men to carry things. Lord Ravell's levy needs archers, and pays sixpence a day when it marches, which is never. Both are roads that sometimes lead up, if a boy is useful and lucky and not too proud.

Your father says the levy made him what he was. He does not say what that was.

- Take service in Sir Hamon Darrell's household, as a groom.
- Take the levy's penny and go north with Sir Ancel Brome's archers.

**Chose:** Take the levy's penny and go north with Sir Ancel Brome's archers.

Will Cobb is Sir Ancel's serjeant now. He writes your name on his roll and tells you the March is cold, the pay is late, and you will be shot at by spring.

*Station: Retainer*

## Leaving

*Spring, year 16 of King Aldred*

You are 15, and you leave in the morning.

You go north as an archer in Sir Ancel's company, at the bottom of the roll.

Your father gives you his bracer, the old one, worn through at the wrist. He holds your arm a moment too long. Mariot Wood is waiting where the chase meets the road, which is not where she lives.

- Go to Mariot and tell her you will come back.
- Promise your father you will find Will Cobb and keep the company's good name.
- Leave before dawn, before anyone can make you promise anything.

**Chose:** Go to Mariot and tell her you will come back.

She says you will not, and that it does not matter, and kisses you as if it does. She gives you a horn button off her own coat. It is the only thing you carry north that is not useful.

## Ravell Hall

You are 15, and you have never seen so many people in one place who were not at a fair.

You see Ravell Hall first from the ridge road, a mile off, across the water meadows: a square grey keep with a timber hall built against its flank like a barn leaning on a church, a curtain wall, a gatehouse, a moat that catches the light. Smoke from a dozen chimneys. Around the walls, outside them, the village: the mill, the smithy, the church of Saint Ebba with its squat tower, the alehouse with a bush over the door. Strip fields running down to the river. Sheep everywhere.

Up close it smells. The moat is green and takes the castle's slops, and in spring it ripens. The gate passage is dark and wet and full of carts. And then you are through, into the outer ward, and it is like being dropped into a hive.

Grooms leading horses two at a time. A cartload of firewood being unloaded by men who swear at it. Laundresses carrying baskets the size of coffins. A falconer with a hooded bird on his fist, walking slowly so as not to unsettle it. Two boys in good cloth fighting with wooden swords while a grey old knight, missing two fingers on his left hand, tells them both they are dead. Hounds. Chickens. A priest. A man in a fur-trimmed gown shouting at a man in a plain one about candles. Nobody looks at you, because everyone here is going somewhere and you are not yet anyone.

*(Continue)*

Lord Thurstan Ravell holds this castle and eleven manors from the King. He holds four knights' fees under him: four knights who owe him forty days' service a year, mounted and armed, in return for their land. Sir Hamon Darrell of Ashby and Sir Ancel Brome of Brome Tower are two of those four. Their households live under his roof for half the year, eat his bread, and fight his quarrels. Yours will too.

Sir Ancel meets you in the yard without getting down from his horse. He is lean as a hayrake, burned dark by the March wind, with half his left ear gone to a Caldmoor sword at Harrow Ford and grey coming into his beard. He looks at you for a long time. "You're late," he says. You are not late; you came the day you were told. You do not say so. Something in his face eases, slightly, at your not saying so. "Stables," he says. "Then find Cobb. Then find me."

*(Continue)*

You will sleep in the long barn with Sir Ancel's archers when the company is at Ravell, which is half the year, and in a ditch on the March when it is not. Will Cobb, the serjeant, a grey-bearded man with a voice like a millrace and hands like roots, gives you a blanket and the worst place by the door, where the wind comes under. "Everyone starts by the door," he says. "Some of 'em die there." He means it as a joke. Then he looks at you again, longer. "Hugh Fletcher's boy. God's teeth. You've his shoulders." He gives you a second blanket and does not explain.

That night you lie awake listening to the castle: a dog barking in the outer ward, the watch calling the hours, someone singing badly in the hall below, a woman laughing somewhere, rain on the roof. You think about home. You think about how far down you are, here, and how many people stand between you and anything. You count them, because counting is what you do. You lose count somewhere past forty.

- Kneel to your master in the yard, properly, as if you had been doing it all your life. *(Risky)*
- Go straight to the stables and make yourself useful before anyone tells you to.
- Find someone in the hall who might tell you how things are done here.

**Chose:** Go straight to the stables and make yourself useful before anyone tells you to.

Nobody has told you what to do, so you do the first thing that needs doing, which is the stables, because there is always something in a stable.

By Vespers you have mucked out three stalls, carried water for twelve horses, found the farrier, held a kicking cob while he shod it, and learned which of your master's horses bites. (The grey. Always the grey.) You have also learned the name of Master Ranulf, the marshal of Ravell, a narrow grey-whiskered man in a leather apron who has run these stables for thirty years and who watches you all afternoon without speaking.

At Compline he stops beside you, looks at the stall you have just cleaned, and kicks the straw to see what is underneath. There is nothing underneath. He nods once and walks away.

Your master hears about it at supper. Sir Ancel says nothing. But the next morning, when the horses are brought out, he hands you the grey's bridle without a word, and you understand that you have passed something.

*Sir Ancel Brome's affection +1 · Master Ranulf's respect +1*

## The Butts at Brome

*Summer, year 16 of King Aldred*

Midsummer on the March. Sir Ancel's company practises at the butts below Brome Tower every morning, and Will Cobb watches who shoots and who talks. Today Sir Ancel is watching too, with a stranger: Sir Walter Pryce, the Earl of Carrow's captain, who is buying archers for somebody's war.

- Shoot slow and steady and say nothing. *(Even)*
- Shoot fast. The Caldmoor men will not wait for you to aim. *(Even)*
- Watch the others closely and do exactly what the best of them does. *(Even)*

**Chose:** Shoot slow and steady and say nothing. (Even: success)

Eleven in twelve. Will Cobb nods once. Sir Walter Pryce asks Sir Ancel what he wants for you, as a joke, and Sir Ancel does not laugh.

*Sir Ancel Brome's respect +1 · Sir Walter Pryce's respect +1*

## Giles Marrick

*Autumn, year 16 of King Aldred*

Giles Marrick is seventeen, the son of a Carrow knight, and he has decided you are a churl in a borrowed coat. He calls you the veteran archer's son whenever he passes. Yesterday he had you whipped for insolence because you did not step out of the mud fast enough when he rode by.

The other squires watch to see what you do.

- Challenge him at the pell, in front of Sir Bertram Oakes. *(Favorable)*
- Arrange for Giles to be found where he should not be. *(Risky)*
- Win over the other squires, one at a time, until Giles is the one who stands alone. *(Risky)*
- ~~Stand the whole loft a cask of Gascon wine on Saint Michael's night.~~ *(Requires Coin 5s)*

**Chose:** Challenge him at the pell, in front of Sir Bertram Oakes. (Favorable: success)

Wasters, not swords, but a waster breaks fingers. You break two of his. Sir Bertram Oakes stops it and says, to the yard in general, that birth is a fine thing and so is keeping your guard up. Giles does not speak to you for a month. When he does, it is with care.

## Winter at the Pell

*Winter, year 16 of King Aldred*

Snow on the moat, ice on the trough, and the hall full of smoke from Martinmas to Candlemas.

Winter is when a household learns. Sir Bertram Oakes takes the young men to the pell every morning before Mass, whatever the weather. Master Ranulf breaks the new horses in the covered ride. Father Benet keeps the lord's accounts and letters in a cold room over the chapel, and will let a boy sit in if he holds his tongue. Will Cobb drills the archers in the barn and the yard and the snow.

You have the winter. You cannot be everywhere.

- Sir Bertram's pell, every morning, until your arms stop shaking.
- The covered ride with Master Ranulf and the young horses.
- Father Benet's cold room, and the lord's accounts.
- Will Cobb's barn, and learning how to make sixty men do one thing.

**Chose:** Will Cobb's barn, and learning how to make sixty men do one thing.

Cobb teaches you to count arrows, call the volleys, and tell which man is drunk before he knows it himself.

## Sir Ancel's Cattle

*Summer, year 17 of King Aldred*

Brome Tower is three days north of Ravell, on the March, where the King's peace is a rumour. In June forty of Sir Ancel's cattle are driven off in the night, and his herdsman is found in the burn with his head stove in.

Everyone knows who did it. Sir Godric Thorne holds the next manor down the valley and has been at law with Sir Ancel for eleven years over a mill. Sir Ancel says he will ride to Thorne's mill tonight and burn it with Thorne's miller inside.

Lord Ravell has written that there will be no private war on the March this year, by the King's order. Sir Ancel has used the letter to light the fire.

- Ride out at first light and follow the cattle yourself. *(Even)*
- ~~Offer to write out Sir Ancel's plaint to Lord Ravell's court instead.~~ *(Risky, Requires: can read)*
- Arm yourself and ride with him to Thorne's mill. *(Favorable)*
- Hold the horses and keep your own counsel.

**Chose:** Ride out at first light and follow the cattle yourself. (Even: success)

The trail does not go to Thorne's manor. It goes north, over the border, by the old drove road. At a shieling past the Leven you find a Caldmoor cattle-buyer's mark cut on a gatepost, and a Thorne man's knife in the mud. Thorne did not take the cattle for himself. He sold them to Black Ewan of Glenhallow. That is not a feud. That is treason.

*Sir Ancel Brome's respect +2 · Woodcraft +1*

## Twelfth Night

*Winter, year 17 of King Aldred*

You are 16.

Twelfth Night at Ravell Hall. The great hall is hung with green, there are minstrels from Wendham, and the Lord of Misrule is a fat cook in a paper crown. Everyone drinks too much. The rules are loosened, not lifted.

Lady Ravell's women dance the carol in a ring and choose partners from the young men. Isabel Sayer is among them. So is Lady Maud de Lisle, Lord Ravell's ward, fifteen, heiress of the barony of Lisle, who is watched by everyone and spoken to by almost no one. The Wendham burgesses have a table below the salt, with their wives and daughters. Master Osbert Fuller's daughter Cecily is the one doing her father's talking for him.

At the top table the knights are drinking a health to the King's claim on Valdrenne, whatever that is going to mean.

- Join the carol and dance with Isabel Sayer. *(Risky)*
- Carry Lady Maud's cup when her own page is drunk, and listen to her. *(Risky)*
- Go below the salt and sit with the Wendham burgesses. *(Even)*

**Chose:** Drink with the knights at the top table, and keep up. (Even: success)

You match Sir Bertram cup for cup and are still standing when he is singing. Your master tells the story of it for a year. The knights do not think of you as one of them. They think of you as someone they drank with, which is a start.

## The Leven Ford

Dawn on the fourth day, in a cold mist off the river. Black Ewan's men are driving the cattle across the Leven ford, slowly, because cattle will not be hurried through water. Eighty men, perhaps more, half of them on the far bank already. They have not seen you.

Sir Ancel draws up the horse behind a fold of ground. Will Cobb has the archers strung and waiting in the alders. Sir Hamon sits the Ravell banner and looks old in the grey light. Beside him is Sir Hugh Wyck, a knight of Lord Ravell's from the next valley, who smiles at you as if this were a hunt.

Sir Ancel says the horse will go in when the archers have loosed twice. That is all the plan there is.

You have no armour. A ford fight is a lottery for a man without it.

- Take a spear and go into the ford with the horse. *(Favorable, MORTAL DANGER)*
- Take two of Cobb's men upstream to the second ford and get behind the herd. *(Favorable)*
- Get down into the alders with Cobb's archers and make them shoot faster. *(Favorable)*
- Wade out and shout for a parley. The cattle, for their lives. *(Risky)*
- Stay with the horses, as you were told.

**Chose:** Get down into the alders with Cobb's archers and make them shoot faster. (Favorable: success)

Six volleys instead of two. The far bank is a mess of screaming horses before the knights even move. When the horse goes in, there is not much left to fight. Will Cobb tells Sir Ancel whose voice was calling the volleys. Only one knight dies at the ford, Sir Hugh Wyck, and he dies because he went in early.

*Renown +2 · Will Cobb's respect +2 · Sir Ancel Brome's respect +1 · Command +1 · Lord Thurstan Ravell's respect +2*

## Home from the Leven

Lord Ravell meets the column at the gate. He counts the cattle, then the men, then the dead. One knight, Sir Hugh Wyck. Two archers. A groom nobody can name.

He stops his horse beside you. "The ford," he says. "That was you." It is not a question. Afterwards, in the hall, he sends you a cup of his own wine from the high table, which every man in the hall sees.

Your master is looking at you as if he were seeing you for the first time.

- Kneel when your master offers to take you as his squire.
- Go back to your duties.

**Chose:** Kneel when your master offers to take you as his squire.

"You'll do," Sir Ancel says. That is the whole ceremony. Grooms and archers do not become squires. You just did.

## The King at Ravell

*Summer, year 19 of King Aldred*

You are 18.

King Aldred comes to Ravell on his summer progress with three hundred people and eats the house bare in four days. He is thirty-four, thin, quick, with his mother's dark Valdrennish eyes. He talks about Valdrenne at every meal. The crown there is his by right, he says, through his mother, and the peers of Valdrenne gave it to a cousin because they were afraid of an Adalian king. He does not say what he means to do about it. Everyone at the high table knows.

The Earl of Carrow rides in on the second day with Sir Walter Pryce and forty men. The Earl does not like the King's war; he likes it less that the King has not asked him to pay for it yet.

The household is in a fever. Squires are everywhere at once. For three days it is possible, just, for anyone to be seen by anyone.

- Get yourself near the King. Hold his stirrup, carry his cup, be there. *(Risky)*
- Watch the King's household for whoever is watching it. *(Risky)*
- Make yourself known to Sir Walter Pryce, who is hiring men for the war. *(Even)*
- Keep to your master's side and do your work well.

**Chose:** Make yourself known to Sir Walter Pryce, who is hiring men for the war. (Even: success)

Sir Walter is a younger son who has made himself a captain and is proud of it. He likes men who came up the hard way, he says, because they know what things cost. He tells you that if you ever need a place, the Earl's company pays on time. He says it as though most companies do not. They do not.

## Sauvemer

Hautbois won the King a battle. It did not win him a port, and without a port on this coast the army goes home and Valdrenne forgets it was ever here.

So in the autumn the army comes to Sauvemer: a walled town on a spit of land between the sea and the marshes, with a double wall, a castle, a harbour full of Valdrennish ships, and a castellan, Sire Enguerrand de Vaux, who sends out a herald to say he has bread for two years and the King of Valdrenne's promise of relief.

*(Continue)*

The King does not storm it. He builds a town of his own outside it, of timber and turf: streets, a market, a church, a hall for himself. The men call it Newtown. He means to sit in it until Sauvemer starves.

It is going to be a long winter, and you have men to keep alive through it.

## The Sortie

*Spring, year 22 of King Aldred*

In the spring the castellan of Sauvemer does what castellans do when they are starving slowly. He comes out.

## The Useless Mouths

*Summer, year 22 of King Aldred*

By summer Sauvemer is eating its horses.

One morning the land gate opens and five hundred people come out: old men, women, children, the sick. The castellan has put out everyone who cannot fight, to save the bread for those who can. They come across the ditch toward the Adalian lines with their hands up.

The King's order comes down at once. Nobody is to pass. If the town wants to feed them, it can take them back. If not, they can stay where they are, between the walls and the lines, and the town can watch.

*(Continue)*

The town does not take them back. They stay in the ditch, in the open, for days. You can hear them at night.

On the third day a woman comes out of the land gate alone, under a white cloth, with a basket: the castellan's wife, Dame Clémence de Vaux, with what bread she can carry. She walks among them handing it out. Then she walks to the Adalian line, to your post, and asks in careful Adalian whether there is any officer here with a Christian heart.

- Let them through your post, the children and the old at least, whatever the King ordered. *(Favorable)*
- Take Dame Clémence's petition to the Queen. The Queen has a reputation for mercy. *(Risky)*
- Send out what bread your company can spare, quietly, at night.
- Obey the order. Keep them out.

**Chose:** Let them through your post, the children and the old at least, whatever the King ordered. (Favorable: failure)

You let the first of them through. Your captain comes down the line at dawn with the provost and stops it, and you are confined to your tent for a week, and the ones still in the ditch stay in the ditch.

## The Relief That Did Not Come

At the end of July, King Amaury comes at last. His army appears on the heights above the marshes: banners, tents, the whole great host of Valdrenne, rebuilt since Hautbois.

And sits there. The only road to Sauvemer runs along the dunes, between the sea and the marsh, and the King has fortified it, and there is no other way. For four days the two armies look at each other. Amaury sends heralds to offer battle on any field of the King's choosing. The King replies that he is quite comfortable where he is.

*(Continue)*

On the fifth morning the Valdrennish heights are empty. Amaury has gone home. In Sauvemer, on the walls, you can see the people who were watching for him, still watching.

In the meantime there have been skirmishes along the dunes, every day, between young men on both sides who want a name.

## The Keys of Sauvemer

Sauvemer surrenders in August, after eleven months. Sire Enguerrand de Vaux does not live to give up his keys. He dies of a fever the night before, and his wife, Dame Clémence, carries them out of the land gate in his place.

The King has been made to wait eleven months and has lost more men to the flux than to the Valdrennish. He demands six burghers of the town, barefoot, in their shirts, with halters round their necks, to give up the keys and to be hanged for the rest.

The six come out. They are old men, mostly, and one young one who volunteered in his father's place. The Queen is there. So are you.

## The Truce of Saint-Lys

*Autumn, year 22 of King Aldred*

The Pontiff's legate, Cardinal Orsino Brancale, a soft-spoken Sarenzan with a merchant's eyes, has been riding between the two kings since Hautbois. ## The Garrison

*Autumn, year 23 of King Aldred*

A truce is a strange thing to live in. You are a conqueror in a conquered country, which sounds grand. In fact it is boredom, cold, short pay, and the feeling of being watched.

The Sauvemer people have not forgiven the siege and will not, in your lifetime. The Adalian garrison drinks, gambles, marries local girls who would rather not, quarrels with the townsmen, and sometimes kills one. The townsmen sometimes kill one back. Every month there is a hanging.

*(Continue)*

Your following has changed since Saltcombe. It is bigger: men drift to a captain who pays. Davy Ludd is nearly eighteen and taller than you.

You have time, for the first time in years, to choose what to do with it.

- Train your men properly, every day, as if the war will start again tomorrow.
- Learn the town. The language, the people, who owes whom.
- Spend your evenings where the women of rank are.
- Put your money to work. Cloth, wine, ransoms: there is money to be made in a garrison town by a man who can count. *(Risky)*

**Chose:** Train your men properly, every day, as if the war will start again tomorrow.

It will start again. Everyone knows the truce will end. When it does, your men will be the best-drilled company in Sauvemer, and they will know it, and so will the men who give out grants.

## The Iron Company

*Summer, year 24 of King Aldred*

In the second summer of the truce the Iron Company comes over the eastern border: eight hundred Hroswald mercenaries under a captain called Ulric Rotbart, whom both kings paid during the war and neither king is paying now.

A free company without a war is a plague with a banner. It takes a village a week and a town a month. The Valdrennish crown will not fight it, because it might be useful later. The Adalian garrison will not fight it, because there is a truce.

## News from the South

*Winter, year 25 of King Aldred*

That winter the Sarenzan galleys come into Sauvemer harbour late, and fewer of them, and the sailors bring a story.

In the south, they say, in the ports of the Midsea, there is a sickness. It begins with a swelling under the arm or in the groin, black, the size of an egg.
