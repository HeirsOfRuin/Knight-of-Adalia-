## Layer 1: The spine

### 1. The dramatic question
*Can the house build higher than it can pay for, and who is still standing when the bank fails?*

Book I asked whether the heir could hold what the founder won. Book II's head inherits a house that held, and wants it to be more: a great marriage, new walls, a town, a place in the war, a claim. All of it is bought with Lanzi credit (B1-2). Acts I-II are the rise. Acts III-IV are the reckoning: the Lanzi crash, the Schism and the commons' rising test everything that was built on borrowed money (B1-4). Whether "the Builder" is an honour or an irony is decided by play.

### 2. The braid
**The realm: war, then the bill for it.** Adalia and Valdrenne go to war again (years 80-86), fought through the West. The crown wants the house's men and money, and pays for the war on credit too. When the Lanzi fail (86-88) on a royal default, the crowns tax what they cannot borrow, and the commons rise against the tax (92-95).

| Frame | The realm strand |
|---|---|
| Free | The West's first war as a realm: Valdrenne over the hills, Adalia watching. Queen Jehanne (or King Gaucelin) needs the great houses. |
| Adalian | Aldred III's war against Valdrenne, fought through the West, with the West's liberties as the price of the West's men. |
| Crowned | The house is the crown. The war, the credit and the tax are the house's own decisions, and the rising is against the house. |

**The rival: the house of Kerguen** (B1-1). The friend of Book I, risen all through it, now a great house in its own right.
- **Who they are.** Tanguy de Kerguen is 49 in year 78. In many runs a Kerguen married into the house in Book I (Azenor to the Keeper; Maëlle to the crowned Keeper; Tanguy to a queen), so the rival is kin. On some crown-lost paths Tanguy is King.
- **Why they are hard.** The house owes Kerguen (Act II's ally in the law, the Armance's cousin). Kerguen has outgrown the house's patronage and no longer needs its leave. It does not hate the house. It wants the same things.
- **The quarrel** (B1-5): **the match, then the debt.** In Act I both houses court the same great match, and one of them loses it. After the crash (Act III) Kerguen buys the house's Lanzi bonds cheap and becomes its creditor: the rival holds the house's paper, and can call it in, forgive it, or sell it.
- **Penhoët** stays a side thread: Sir Yvon of Kerlan's claim if it was broken, kin if it was reconciled, a treaty if it was contained. It returns as a power in Book III (PLAN.md §6.6, "the last Penhoët").

**The family: the Builder and the debt** (B1-2). The head's own conflict is credit. Every great thing in Book II has a price in pounds and a bond in Sauvemer:
- the great match (a dowry, a jointure, a feast);
- works that take years to pay back (walls, a bridge, a market town, a castle);
- the war (men raised, ransoms paid or taken);
- a claim, where the match carries one.
The Lanzi lend gladly until they cannot. In the crash a house with bonds out is called in, and a house that lent to the crown loses it.

**Where they touch.** The war is paid for on credit, by the crowns and by the house. Kerguen rises in the war, as the crown's captain or its lender. When the bank fails, whoever is most in debt falls furthest, and whoever holds the debts holds the West. The rising is the commons presenting the bill for all of it.

### 5. The paths
- **Founder path, free and Adalian frames.** The Builder is the Keeper's heir. Beat sheets are written for these two frames first, as in Book I.
  - **The great match, free West** (B1-6): **Jehanne's child**, her eldest (about ten in 940), betrothed young and married at the act's end. It brings the royal line or the Armance's succession, and the claim Book III's war of cousins needs. Kerguen, Jehanne's cousin, wants the same match. Under King Gaucelin the match is Gaucelin's heir; where the Armance went elsewhere in Book I, it is the Armance's heir.
  - **The great match, Adalian West** (PLAN.md §6.3): an Adalian earldom, with Wendmere's leave. Layer 2 names it.
- **Crowned path.** The Builder is a child sovereign under a regency (B1-3): the mother is regent by law; the retired sovereign is a second power at court who never quite let go. Act I opens with that struggle and ends at the Builder's sixteenth birthday (about 942). The Builder is played from the start.
  - **Where the retired sovereign is the mother** (B1-9; the author's second save, where Queen Ysolde crowns her daughter Agnes and steps down): the regent and the old sovereign are one person, so the second power is **the child's father**, the retired sovereign's consort (Tanguy de Kerguen in that run, so Kerguen sits in the regency itself). If the father is dead, **the Estates elect** a regent or a council, and the minority is the crown against the Estates.
- **Crown-lost runs.** The house is the greatest in the West under another king, with a claim. Book II reads the claim (designated, renounced or quiet) from Book I's N12.
- **Divided frame and the other openings.** Sketched at act level in Layer 2, as Book I's were.

### 6. What Book I hands on
Read at the start of Book II: the house's standing, purse, debt and holdings; Penhoët's settlement; who holds the Armance; the crown's law (hereditary, for life, defied); the Builder's upbringing and bond; the old Keeper's manner of leaving (hall, cloister, junior crown, abdication, death); Kerguen's standing and temper; any match into Kerguen, Penhoët or Valdrenne; the bishop; the ward.

### 7. Systems Book II needs (flagged, not designed here)
- **Works:** building that costs for years before it pays (walls, a bridge, a town charter, a castle), replacing Book I's one-off purchases. This is also the answer to Book I's hoarded purses (£4,000-5,000 on the Founder path, £12,000-13,000 on the crowned).
- **Credit:** larger Lanzi loans, bonds with terms, the crown as a borrower from the house.
- **The crash:** loans called in, deposits lost, a crown's default.
- **The war at realm scale:** levies, seasons in the field, ransoms, and the war's effect on trade (built small in Book I).
- **Kerguen as an active rival:** a standing race, and Kerguen buying what the house sells.
- **The Schism:** the house's obedience, and the Church reputations it moves.
- **The rising**, and **the frame change** in Act IV (B1-7): one change at most. An Adalian West can break free if the rising wins and the house leads or backs it; a free West can be partly conquered if the war and the crash leave it broke; a crowned house can lose the crown to the rising. Each fork carries into Book III.
- **The Builder's end** (B1-8): the last scene always ends the headship, as in Book I, shaped by Acts III-IV: killed in the rising, broken by the debt (selling up, flight, the cloister), or handing on a house that held.

## Layer 2: The acts (decided 2026-10-10/11)

**The chain.** Book II is one chain of cause and effect: the war is fought on credit (Act II), a crown defaults on it and the Lanzi fail (Act III), the crowns tax what they can no longer borrow, and the commons rise against the tax (Act IV). Each act's likely outcome stands unless a strong house overturns it, as in Book I.

**The years, adjusted from PLAN.md §5** (the master timeline allows it): the war 81-86 (943-948), the crash 87 (949), the Schism 89 (951), the tax 90-92, the rising 93-95 (955-957), Amaury VII's "absences" from 96 (958), the Builder's end 100-102 (962-964).

### Act I: *The Great Match* (years 78-82, 940-944)
**Leans on:** the family and the rival. **The realm shows as:** the war coming.

| Path | Premise |
|---|---|
| Free, Founder | Jehanne's eldest (about nine in 940) is the great match (B1-6); Kerguen courts the same child for Tanguy's grandson. Under King Gaucelin it is his heir; where the Armance went elsewhere, the Armance's heir. |
| Adalian, Founder | An Adalian earldom, with Wendmere's leave: the Haverell heir (Lord Edmund Haverell's line, from Book I's E1), with Aldred III's goodwill as the dowry. Kerguen wants an Adalian earldom too. |
| Crowned | The minority first (B1-3, B1-9): the regent and the second power fight for the child until the Builder is sixteen (942 to 945 by run). Then the crown's own match (B2-5): **Valdrenne's line**, Amaury VII's granddaughter or grandson, a peace match made just before the war, so the war of Act II is fought against the in-laws. Kerguen, the minority's power, opposes it. |
| Crown lost | The claim (from Book I's N12) and the match: a marriage into the new king's line would fold the claim into the crown. If the king is Tanguy, it is Kerguen's line. |

**Turning points:**
1. The house's first works on credit: the Lanzi's factor comes to the Builder, as he came to the Keeper, with more in his ledger (works system).
2. The courtship: the house and Kerguen at the same door, with gifts, dowry offers and the child's guardians weighing both.
3. The decision: the match is made or lost. If lost, Kerguen has it, and the grievance runs the rest of the book.
4. The wedding, or the betrothal kept for the child's majority; the war declared at its end (943).

**Choices that matter:**
- how much the house borrows to win the match (dowry, jointure, gifts, the feast);
- whether it wins the match, and against Kerguen or with Kerguen bought off;
- (crowned) whom the young sovereign trusts in the minority, and how the regency ends.

**Carries forward:** the match and its claim; the first bonds; Kerguen's temper and standing; (crowned) the regency's end and the Estates' mood.

**Pool:** the Lanzi's factor; a rival suitor's insult; a bad harvest; the bookish child and the tutor.

### Act II: *The Foreign War* (years 82-87, 944-949)
**Leans on:** the realm. **The rival shows as:** Kerguen, the crown's captain (B2-3).

| Frame | Premise |
|---|---|
| Free | The West's first war as a realm: Amaury VII, fifty-eight, comes over the hills for what he lost in 905, and Adalia sells help at a price. The crown summons the houses. |
| Adalian | Aldred III's war against Valdrenne, fought through the West. The West's men are the price of the West's liberties, and the patent is read again. |
| Crowned | The house's own war: the crown raises the host, borrows for it at the crown's scale, and the Builder commands it, or the constable does. |
| Crown lost | The house is the king's greatest vassal, and the king's greatest worry: its men, or its money, or its neutrality. |

**Turning points:**
1. The summons: serve in person, send the house's men, or pay scutage; each on borrowed money.
   - **Penhoët returns in the war** (B2-8): if broken, Sir Yvon of Kerlan's claim surfaces with Valdrenne behind it; if reconciled, young Yann (about twenty-five) rides with the house; if contained, Penhoët chooses its side by how the house has treated it.
2. The first campaign: a march castle besieged; the war system at realm scale.
3. Kerguen's rise: the constableship, the glory, the ransoms. Kerguen comes out of the war rich (B2-3).
4. The field: one battle that the house's choices weigh on; the Builder can be taken, ransomed, wounded.
5. The peace of 948: **a ruinous draw** by default (B2-1). Both crowns are broke. A strong house can tip it into a real win: more glory, and more debt.

**Choices that matter:** serve, send or pay; how much to borrow; ransoms taken or mercy given; whether the house stands with Kerguen in the field or against it for command.

**Carries forward:** the house's debt and renown; the war's outcome (draw, win, or a lost march); Kerguen's ransoms; prisoners owed.

**Pool:** a ransom owed; the free company paid off; a widow's dower suit; the war's harvest.

### Act III: *Debt and the Schism* (years 87-93, 949-955)
**Leans on:** the family (the debt) and the rival (the paper). **The realm shows as:** the Schism and the tax.

**Turning points:**
1. **The default** (949): Amaury VII's crown repudiates its war debts, and the Lanzi fail (B2-2). On the crowned path the house's own crown can default first, by choice: its treasury is saved, the bank is broken, and every lender in the West knows whose fault it was.
2. **The bonds sold** (950): the Lanzi's receivers sell what the house owes. Kerguen buys the house's paper cheap (B1-5), and can call it in, forgive it for a price, or sell it to the house's enemies. A house that lent to a crown loses it.
3. **The Schism** (951): two Popes. The Estates (free) or the King with the West's bishops (Adalian) choose, and **the house tips it** with its vote and its bishop (B2-4). Saint-Lys means Valdrenne's Pope and peace on the march; Sarenza means the bankers' Pope and relief on Sarenzan debt.
4. **The tax** (952-954): the crowns tax what they cannot borrow (a poll tax in Adalia, the salt tithe in the West, a hearth tax on the crowned path). The house collects it on its own lands, or remits it and pays the difference.

**Choices that matter:** pay Kerguen, bargain, or defy; sell works, lands or a child's marriage to clear the paper; the obedience; collect the tax or carry it.

**Carries forward:** the house's debt to Kerguen; what was sold; the obedience; the commons' temper on the house's lands and in the realm.

**Pool:** a preacher of the other Pope; a merchant's ruin; a bad harvest in a tax year; a pilgrim's relic (low magic).

### Act IV: *The Rising* (years 93-100, 955-962), and the Builder's end (100-102)
**Leans on:** the realm, then the family.

| Frame | Premise |
|---|---|
| Free | The commons rise against the salt tithe. The crown wants the house's men. If the war and the crash left the West broke, Valdrenne or Adalia comes for a piece of it: the **part-conquest** fork (B1-7). |
| Adalian | The poll tax; Wendmere's governors hanged at Lannec. **The breakaway** fork: if the rising wins, and the house leads or backs it, the West can be free (B1-7). |
| Crowned | The rising is against the house's own tax. Ride out to meet them, crush them, or concede. The crown can be lost to it (B1-7). |
| Crown lost | The rising is against the king the Estates chose instead of the house. The house's claim is the rising's banner, if the house will let it be. |

**Turning points:**
1. The rising (955): a commons captain, a Coker if Wat Coker's line is alive in the export (Knight of Adalia's promise), with the house's own tenants among the marchers.
2. The meeting: the house rides out to them, or the crown does, or nobody does.
3. The verdict of the rising: crushed, conceded, or won, and the frame change if it comes (one at most).
4. Amaury VII's absences (958): Valdrenne's king is sometimes mad, and the West's neighbours weaken.
5. **The Builder's end** (962-964), always taken (B1-8): dead in the rising, broken by the debt (selling up, flight, the cloister), or handing on a house that held.

**Choices that matter** (B2-6): in every frame the house can **lead** the rising (in an Adalian West, the breakaway; in a free West, the commons' champion against the crown), **crush** it for the crown (favour, and the commons' hatred), or **broker** terms by riding out to meet the marchers. The house's choice decides whether the frame changes. Then, how the Builder ends.

**Carries forward into Book III:** the frame; the claim; Kerguen's hold on the house; the obedience; the Builder's heir and bond; the war of cousins' first causes.

**Pool:** the old company's last kin; a lord's castle burned; a hanged steward; a peace bought with a daughter.

### Knight of Adalia promises Book II keeps
- **A Coker leads the rising** where Wat Coker's line is alive in the export ("dies free on his own holding").
- **A bookish child writes the chronicle**: the Chronicle panel is "by" a bookish child of the Keeper or the Builder.
- **The peace's length:** `c5r_peace_bought` holds to year 75 and `c5r_peace_marriage` to about 70, so the war of 81 breaks neither.
