### The King at Lannec

*Spring 912, the seventh year of King Arnaud*

The great hall at Lannec is cold in the mornings, and so are you. You are forty-nine. The fire was lit at Prime and has not reached the dais yet, and the chamberlain stands at your elbow with the day's petitions under his arm, waiting for you to be ready.

You were a wool merchant's son once. Now the hall stands when you come in, and the hall is the hall where the Estates of the West made you king, and every morning you walk past the place where you stood that day and do not look at it.

Mahaut comes down with her women. She is thirty-one, and Queen of the West, and Duchess of Armance in her own right, which she has never once let a clerk forget. She takes the chair beside yours and moves the salt so you can reach it. Jehanne comes after her at a run, six years old, in a gown she has already torn, and climbs into your lap without asking, as she has since she could climb. The younger ones come behind with their nurse, in order of size.

"Your daughter," says Mahaut, "has been in the stables again."

"My daughter," you say, "is going to be Queen of the West. She can be in the stables if she likes."

You ride out by the river gate with Jehanne in front of you on the saddle, holding the reins she thinks are steering the horse. The people on the road stop and bow and stare. ### The Illness

*Winter 914, the ninth year of King Arnaud*

It comes at Christmas, with the great hall full and the boar's head on the table. A shaking first, that you put down to the cold. Then a heat, and a weight on your chest like a man kneeling on it.

You wake in your own bed with no memory of getting there. Mahaut is sitting beside it in her furs with a book of accounts on her knee, and turns a page, and looks up when you move, and you see on her face what you look like. Jehanne is asleep at the foot of the bed in a nest of cloaks, with her shoes on.

The fever goes in a week. The weakness does not. You cannot get up the stairs without stopping. You cannot sit a horse.

"Well," says Davy, from the door. "You look like a thing the dogs dug up."

"There is a Sarenzan physician in the town," says Mahaut, "who cured a bishop. There is the relic of Saint Gildas at the abbey. And there is lying still and doing what you're told." She closes the book. "I would choose the third. You won't."

**You choose:** Lie still and do as Mahaut says, for once.

You lie still. Mahaut runs the kingdom from a chair beside your bed for the whole of Lent, and runs it well, and tells you about it every evening whether you want to hear or not. It is the first winter in thirty years you have not ridden out. The West, you notice, does not fall down.

### The Crowning

*Spring 915, the tenth year of King Arnaud*

Lady Day, the new year. You sit in the gallery in a chair with a fur over your knees, and you know.

You will live. The physicians say so, and you believe them, and you can feel it: you will sit in the sun for years yet. But you will not ride to the march again, or sit a whole day's assize, or hold a great council by the force of standing up in it. The kingdom cannot wait for you to get better. It can only wait for Jehanne to grow up.

The kings of Valdrenne used to do it this way, in the old days, when they were afraid: crown the heir while the father lived, so that there was never a day without a crowned head. Jehanne is nine. Her brothers and sisters are younger. Mahaut would govern for her. You would be there, behind the throne, for as long as you last.

The Estates swore to "the King's heir, as the law shall be". That will not help. Bishop Évrard of Saint-Lys, who crowned you, is a hundred and one, and has sent word that he would like to crown one more, if it is soon.

- Crown her at Saint-Lys, before the Estates and all the West.
- Crown her quietly in the palace chapel, and tell the West after.
- Crown her, and name her council in the same hour, so no one else can.

**You choose:** Crown her at Saint-Lys, before the Estates and all the West.

They carry you into the cathedral in a chair, and you stand for the crowning, because you will not sit for it. Old Évrard sets a small crown on Jehanne's head with hands that shake so badly two canons have to steady them, and the oldest man in the West crowns the youngest, and the nave roars. Jehanne does not cry, and does not smile, and does not drop the sceptre, which is heavier than she is. Afterwards, in the sacristy, she is sick into a basin, and then asks for her dinner.

### The Council

*Summer 915, the first year of Queen Jehanne*

You are nine years old, and Queen of the West, and the chair in the council chamber was made for your father. Your feet do not reach the floor. Somebody has put a footstool under them, tactfully, and you would like to kick it across the room.

Your mother sits at the other end of the table, and governs. She is the regent. That is a word you have learned this spring, along with "minority", "the Great Seal", and "Your Grace", which is what the chancellor calls you now, every time, as if checking it still works.

### The Regency

*Autumn 915, the first year of Queen Jehanne*

After Michaelmas your mother sends for you to her own chamber, with only her old nurse present, the way she once sent for your father when they were deciding something nobody could know yet.

### The King's Question

*Spring 916, the second year of Queen Jehanne*

In the spring your father is well enough to sit in the garden at the foot of the palace wall, in the sun, with a blanket and a cup of wine he is not supposed to have.

You sit with him most mornings, after council. Today he talks. He has not talked like this in your life. About where he came from: a wool merchant's son, with nothing, in a year nobody remembers. About your mother, the garden at Lannec, the hawk on her fist that she did not need. About the war, and the bridge, and the Estates, and the morning they made him king and he stood in the hall and could not feel his own feet.

Then he stops, and looks at you, and you can see him decide to say it.

"You're ten," he says. "You've had a year of it. Bridges and councils and your mother." He takes your hand. His is dry and light. "Tell me. Are you going to be me?"

**You choose:** "No. I'm going to be me."

He laughs, which hurts him, and grips your hand. "Good," he says. "Good. One of me was plenty."

### The Acclamation

*Spring 917, the third year of Queen Jehanne*

Lady Day, 917. You are eleven. The Estates of the West meet at Lannec to acclaim you, in person, as their Queen: not as a child with a crown on, but as the Queen they will obey.

## Book One: The Keeper

### The Homage of the Lords

*Summer 917, the third year of Queen Jehanne*

At midsummer the great lords of the West come to Lannec to do you homage in person, now that the Estates have acclaimed you: every man who holds of the crown, one by one, on his knees, with his hands between yours.

Your hands are small. The lords' hands are not. By the twentieth you have stopped noticing whose they are, and by the fortieth your mother, standing behind your chair, has started saying the names to you under her breath before each man reaches the dais, so that you can say them first and watch his face.

Hervé de Penhoët comes near the end. He is a careful man of fifty in good dark wool, with a clerk behind him and no sword. He kneels, and puts his hands between yours, and says the words, all of them, clearly, and then three more.

"Saving the right of my house," he says, "in the duchy of Armance."

Behind you, your mother has gone completely still.

**You choose:** Tell him to swear plain, or not at all.

### Penhoët's Claim

*Autumn 917, the third year of Queen Jehanne*

After Michaelmas Hervé de Penhoët puts it in writing, before the council, in his own neat hand.

The duchy of Armance, he says, descended by the male line for three hundred years, until the war gave it to a woman. His house is the old line's nearest male heir. Your mother holds it for her life; he does not dispute that; he is a loyal man. But at her death, he says, it should come to Penhoët by the custom of the Armance, and not to her daughter, who is Queen of the West already and has a crown to be going on with.

Then, at the bottom, under his seal, the offer. His son Ronan is twenty-one. If the Queen were to marry Ronan, the claim and the duchy would be in one bed, and there would be no quarrel to speak of, ever again.

"It's a good letter," says the chancellor, unhappily.

"It's an excellent letter," says your mother. "That is what I don't like about it."

**You choose:** Hear the offer. Ask to meet Ronan de Penhoët.

### The Salt Towns' Charter

### Before the Estates

*Spring 920, the sixth year of Queen Jehanne*

## Book One, Act Two: The Law

> A crown is a promise the Estates made once, and are asked to make again every time it changes heads.

### The Estates Sit

*Summer 920, the sixth year of Queen Jehanne*

At Whitsun in 920 the Estates of the West sit at Lannec, in the hall where your father was made king and you were acclaimed: the lords on their benches, the bishops in their stalls, the burgesses of the salt towns at the back. Before the first session Hervé de Penhoët asks for an hour, and gets it, in your mother's solar, with your mother in the room. He says it again, plainly: Penhoët's voice, and the voices of the Armance lords who go with Penhoët, for the queen's cause. Your mother says nothing at all. She is looking at you.

### The Sickness Comes

### The Old King

*Spring 926, the twelfth year of Queen Jehanne*

In Lent of 926 your father takes the Mottle. It comes on him in the night, the fever and the dark mottling of the skin, and in the morning the physicians come out of his chamber and will not look at you.

He asks for you. You are in the palace, three doors down the gallery. Tanguy begs you not to go, because the sickness takes the young, and you are young.

- Go to him, and sit with him to the end.

**You choose:** Go to him, and sit with him to the end.

You sit with him for three days, with a cloth over your mouth that he makes you take off so he can see your face. On the second night he is clear for an hour.

"You said you'd be yourself," he says. "You were. I watched." He laughs, and it hurts him. "Better than I'd have been at it."

He dies on the third morning, at the hour of Prime, with the bells going for someone else in the city. He was sixty-three.

### The Crown in the Plague

*Summer 926, the twelfth year of Queen Jehanne*

### The Friar

*Summer 927, the thirteenth year of Queen Jehanne*

A friar comes through in the summer with forty people behind him, barefoot, singing, beating their own backs with knotted cords until the blood runs. ### The Crown After the Mottle

*Spring 929, the fifteenth year of Queen Jehanne*

In the spring of 929 the West counts what the Mottle left it. The chancellor brings you the roll in the council chamber: a third of the towns' taxes uncollected, the bridges on the salt road down, two sheriffs dead and nobody appointed, and the Estates, who have not met since before the sickness, asking when they will.

"They'll want thanking," he says. "For the crown's peace. For the crown at all, if I'm honest." He folds the roll. "The Estates gave the crown in 920 on terms, and they read the terms more often than you do."

- Remit the towns' tax for a year, and rebuild the salt road's bridges. (£80)

**You choose:** Summon the Estates, and thank them for the crown's peace.

### Hervé's Letters

### A Preacher in the Market

### Hervé Rises

Hervé does not wait for the Duchess to die.
